# Monochrome East Asian Brush Anime

## Background Economy

- Keep the requested setting recognizable with only a few large, simple shapes. Leave broad quiet areas; do not fill empty space to make the image look complete.
- Include only explicitly requested objects or those indispensable to the action or location. Do not add incidental props, decorations, extra people, or repeated background objects.
- Keep background detail clearly lower than the subject. Do not subdivide large shapes into many small parts, patterns, surface textures, or decorative lighting effects. Preserve explicitly requested scene elements.

## Style and Request

- Create a finished black-and-white Japanese anime illustration drawn in lines with a thick traditional East Asian brush dipped in black ink.
- Follow the requested character identity, age, appearance, clothing, expression, action, props, camera angle, framing, and setting.
- Keep detail density low throughout, even for elaborate characters and scenes.

## Character and Shape

- Use a modestly enlarged head, a compact lower face, and mildly shortened, simplified torso and limbs. Keep joints and gestures coherent.
- Use expressive anime eyes and a small, simply drawn nose and mouth. Match the expression to the request.
- Draw hair as one clear silhouette with only a few major divisions. Omit fine strands, repeated inner strokes, and decorative highlights.
- Draw clothing through its outline and only indispensable structural marks. Leave broad areas blank; omit routine folds, seams, and small fastenings or ornaments.
- Keep eyes, mouth, and hands simply drawn. No intricate irises, decorative lashes, separate lip contours, or detailed anatomy.

## Brush Linework

- Use broad, bold brush lines. Press the thick brush down to make visibly wide strokes, then lift to a pointed end, preserving a strong contrast between broad strokes and fine tips.
- Keep strokes expressive and economical, with simple facial marks. Avoid uniformly thin pen lines and dense hair or fold strokes.
- Use fewer strokes rather than filling shapes with brush marks. Omit secondary details even when the requested design is elaborate.
- Preserve clearly drawn anime forms; do not replace them with broad painterly brush masses.

## Black and White

- Ignore requested hues in monochrome areas. Render dark local colors as solid black and light local colors as unfilled white; do not convert colors into gray values.
- Fills describe the object's own color, not lighting. Do not add form shadows, highlights, or intermediate tones to model volume. Use contours or small white gaps to keep black shapes readable.
- Use only pure black and pure white. No gray fills, screentones, gradients, or soft shading.
- Avoid ink bleeding, diluted ink washes, dry-brush texture, splatters, and distressed paper.

## Scene and Output

- Use a white background by default. Render requested settings and props with a few essential shapes, no denser than the character.
- Do not automatically add traditional clothing, landscape motifs, calligraphy, or seals.
- Add text, borders, or decorative marks only when requested.
- Produce a clear, finished 2D anime drawing with expressive East Asian brush lines, not a traditional ink painting.
