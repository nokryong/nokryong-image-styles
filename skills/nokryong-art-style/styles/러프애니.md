# Understated Rough Anime Drawing

## Style and Request

- Create a light, casual Japanese indie-anime illustration with a finished but slightly rough handmade appearance.
- Keep rendering density low across the entire image, even for elaborate characters or scenes. Do not add detail to make a simple drawing appear more polished.
- Follow the requested character, clothing, expression, action, camera angle, framing, and setting. References define identity; this document defines rendering.

## Deformation Level

- Use the shared moderate deformation level: a visibly but modestly enlarged head, a compact simplified lower face, and mildly shortened, simplified torso and limbs. Keep joints and gestures coherent.
- Keep this degree of proportion exaggeration the same across the standard illustration styles, including commercial vector: stronger than the light deformation of everyday webtoon, clearly milder than deformed manga, and far milder than chibi. Each style retains its own facial design and rendering technique.

## Character and Shape

- Use a short rounded face, a small lower face and chin, comparatively large rounded oval eyes, a small nose, and a very short mouth.
- Keep iris detail simple and concentrate facial information in the eyes. Adapt facial acting to the requested emotion.
- Describe the body, hands, and feet through readable silhouettes rather than detailed muscle modeling.
- Construct hair from a few large unified masses and a few front and side locks, with a slightly irregular outer silhouette. Do not draw individual strands or detailed hair texture.
- Construct clothing from broad flat masses with only a few essential fold shapes. No dense wrinkles, stitching, fabric texture, ornate eye detail, or intricate anatomical rendering.

## Linework

- Use thin, nearly black lines with a lightly wavering handmade quality.
- Keep line weight mostly uniform, allowing mild variation in selected areas.
- Prioritize contours and essential internal divisions; avoid mechanical cleanup and dense detail lines.

## Color and Shading

- Use flat cel coloring and cel shading only: one uniform base color and one shallow, hard-edged shadow step per material. No intermediate tones or additional shadow steps.
- Keep highlights simple and hard-edged, with low contrast and little gloss.
- If blush is appropriate, use small flat color shapes or fine hatching.
- Avoid gradients, airbrushing, soft blending, painterly color mixing, complex textures, realistic or 3D rendering, and a polished commercial finish.

## Scene and Output

- Choose composition for the requested action and camera. Use an empty or simple bright background with only necessary scene cues, rendered as large simple shapes. No detailed scenery or complex incidental objects.
- Keep the whole image low in detail density and consistent with a casual drawing log.
- Produce a finished illustration; add text only when requested.
