# Everyday Japanese Manga Scene

## Style and Request

- Draw an ordinary story panel from a serialized Japanese manga, using economical dip-pen linework on white paper.
- Follow the requested character, age, appearance, body type, clothing, emotion, action, camera, framing, and setting.
- Keep the drawing simple and practical, as part of an ongoing manga chapter.
- Keep detail density low even for elaborate character designs. Omit secondary details rather than drawing them smaller.

## Scene and Acting

- Make the character visibly engaged in the requested situation: speaking, listening, doing something, or reacting to what just happened. When no action is specified, choose one small everyday action suited to the request.
- Connect the gaze, expression, hands, and posture to that action. A quiet reaction or close-up can carry the scene without adding props or other people.
- Frame the moment while it is happening, with natural cropping and unforced acting. Preserve requested camera angles and wallpaper spacing when specified.
- Do not default to posing for the viewer, a decorative head tilt, a hand near the lips, or a beauty-portrait composition unless requested or necessary to the action.

## Character and Linework

- Use simply drawn manga faces and coherent, lightly simplified human forms. Let the expression determine the eye and mouth shapes.
- Draw the mouth as a simple line or flat open shape, without separate lip contours or lip shading. Keep eyes simple, with minimal lashes and iris marks.
- Use a dip pen with natural line-weight variation. Draw only the contours and internal marks needed to read the character and action.
- Draw hair as a clear silhouette with only a few major divisions, without fine strands or repeated internal strokes.
- Describe clothing with its outline and only indispensable structural marks. Leave broad areas blank; omit routine folds, seams, stitching, and minor fastenings or ornaments.
- Simplify hands and anatomy into readable shapes. Do not add fine surface marks or decorative detail to polish the drawing.

## Black and White

- Ignore requested hues in monochrome areas. Render dark local colors as solid black and light local colors as unfilled white; do not convert colors into gray values.
- Fills describe the object's own color, not lighting. Do not add form shadows, highlights, or intermediate tones to model volume. Use contours or small white gaps to keep black shapes readable.
- No screentones, gray fills, hatching, gradients, gloss, color accents, or painterly rendering.
- Keep faces and hair simply drawn rather than polishing them into a monochrome anime illustration.

## Background and Output

- Use only the location cues and objects indispensable to the requested situation. Do not add scenery, decorations, or repeated objects to fill space.
- Keep the background less detailed than the character, with broad quiet areas. Honor a requested plain background.
- Produce one continuous scene. Add panel borders, dialogue, captions, sound effects, or other text only when requested.
