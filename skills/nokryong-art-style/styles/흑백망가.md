# Low-Detail Monochrome Serialized Manga

## Style and Request

- Create a finished black-and-white image with the economical ink treatment of an ordinary panel in a Japanese weekly manga.
- Follow the requested character, age, body type, clothing, emotion, action, camera angle, framing, and setting.
- Preserve the understated, narrative-first finish of a serialized page rather than a detailed cover or promotional illustration.
- Low line count and low information density are mandatory. Elaborate characters or settings must not increase rendering complexity.

## Deformation Level

- Use the shared moderate deformation level: a visibly but modestly enlarged head, a compact simplified lower face, and mildly shortened, simplified torso and limbs. Keep joints and gestures coherent.
- Keep this degree of proportion exaggeration the same across the standard illustration styles, including commercial vector: stronger than the light deformation of everyday webtoon, clearly milder than deformed manga, and far milder than chibi. Each style retains its own facial design and rendering technique.

## Character and Shape

- Preserve coherent joints, hands, and body connections within the shared moderately deformed proportions.
- Preserve natural foreshortening, overlap, and cropping appropriate to the camera.
- Keep eyes minimal and flat with few eyelashes, and use small symbolic noses and mouths.
- Construct hair from a few large unified masses with only essential divisions. No strand-by-strand rendering or fine hair texture.
- Construct clothing from broad flat masses with a few essential folds. Simplify hands and props; no dense wrinkles, stitching, fabric texture, intricate accessory details, or finely modeled anatomy.

## Ink Linework

- Use finished black manuscript lines resembling single-stroke G-pen work.
- Keep contours confident but slightly uneven in pressure, with a low line count and little internal detail.
- Avoid pencil sketching, duplicate contours, construction lines, excessive hatching, and dense rendering.

## Black-and-White Ink and Cel Shading

- Design the image directly in pure black ink on pure white paper. No gray fills, gray screentones, tinted paper, or color accents.
- Do not desaturate a color illustration or convert material colors and lighting into a grayscale range. Use economical contours, white areas, and selected solid-black masses.
- Use binary cel shading only: white base areas and at most one sparse, hard-edged solid-black shadow step. Separate overlapping forms through contours and white gaps.
- Any necessary hatching consists of a few distinct black marks with white gaps, never a dense gray tonal field.
- No gradients, soft blending, grayscale modeling, glossy effects, or decorative shading.

## Scene and Output

- Use a borderless single scene on white paper by default, with large unmarked areas and minimal environmental lines.
- Let the requested action and camera determine composition and perspective, including dynamic poses.
- Keep backgrounds economical without omitting context essential to the scene. No dense scenery, detailed architecture, background screentone or gradients, complex incidental objects, or detailed surface textures.
- Add panel borders, dialogue, captions, sound effects, or other text only when requested.
