# Anime-Influenced Indie Web Cartoon

## Style and Request

- Combine cute Japanese anime facial construction with the bold graphic language of modern Western indie web animation.
- Follow the requested character identity, age, appearance, clothing, proportions, expression, action, props, camera angle, framing, and setting.
- References define character design; apply this illustration style consistently to every character.
- Keep the result playful, energetic, slightly edgy, and unmistakably two-dimensional.
- Low rendering density applies to all characters and scenery. Do not increase rendering complexity in response to elaborate character, costume, or scene descriptions.

## Anime Faces and Simple Mouths

- Use expressive anime-inspired eyes, softly simplified cheeks, expressive eyebrows, and small understated noses.
- Keep eye construction graphic, with bold upper eyelids, limited highlights, and little internal detail.
- Draw closed mouths as small lines and open mouths as flat shapes with minimal interior detail.
- Avoid separately modeled lips, protruding lip volumes, distinct upper and lower contours, lipstick, lip gloss, and realistic mouth shading.
- Keep facial appeal in eye design, expression, and proportions rather than detailed lip rendering.
- Preserve anime facial structure during exaggerated reactions; do not substitute elongated faces, heavily modeled cheekbones, or exaggerated Western cartoon mouth anatomy.
- Allow simple blush marks, tiny fangs, or playful mouth shapes where appropriate to the requested character and expression.

## Deformation Level

- Use the shared moderate deformation level: a visibly but modestly enlarged head, a compact simplified lower face, and mildly shortened, simplified torso and limbs. Keep joints and gestures coherent.
- Keep this degree of proportion exaggeration the same across the standard illustration styles, including commercial vector: stronger than the light deformation of everyday webtoon, clearly milder than deformed manga, and far milder than chibi. Each style retains its own facial design and rendering technique.

## Shapes and Acting

- Combine soft forms and sharp accents within the shared moderate deformation level. Exaggerated acting does not require stronger baseline head-to-body distortion.
- Simplify bodies and limbs while preserving coherent anatomy and recognizable character silhouettes.
- Adapt exaggeration to the requested body type and pose rather than forcing one generic mascot design.
- Use lively gestures and readable facial acting, adjusting emotional intensity to the scene instead of imposing a fixed mischievous expression.
- Avoid realistic anatomical modeling and elongated fashion-illustration proportions.

## Bold Linework

- Use clean expressive black or dark-colored lines, with thick outer contours and simpler, slightly thinner internal marks.
- Vary line weight to clarify expressions, gestures, and overlapping forms.
- Favor confident fluid strokes and crisp screen-readable edges.
- Avoid delicate thin outlining, messy sketch marks, and excessive interior detail.

## Hair, Clothes, and Accessories

- Construct hair from a few large unified masses, chunky locks, and expressive silhouettes, with a few sharp graphic highlights. No individual strands, fine hair texture, or excessive internal divisions.
- Preserve important hairstyles, clothing shapes, symbols, and accessories.
- Construct clothing from broad flat masses with only a few purposeful folds and broad shadow shapes. No dense wrinkles, stitching, fabric texture, ornate patterns, or intricate accessory rendering; simplify essential design motifs.
- Keep detail density consistent across face, hair, clothing, and props.
- Avoid fine hair strands, elaborate gloss, intricate textures, and unnecessary ornament.

## Cel Shading and Color

- Use flat cel coloring and cel shading only: uniform base colors and a single shadow tone per material, arranged into a few broad hard-edged masses. Preserve substantial uninterrupted base-color areas; no intermediate tonal modeling.
- Maintain depth through overlap, clear color separation, and purposeful shadow placement.
- Favor saturated colors and controlled graphic contrast, respecting requested character colors and scene palettes.
- Neon accents or colored rim light may be used when they fit the scene; render light on characters as simplified hard-edged shapes.
- Render background illumination with flat color areas and hard-edged graphic accents. Do not introduce blurred glow, gradients, or soft shading in the background.
- Avoid airbrushing, painterly blending, realistic skin texture, complex tonal modeling, and glossy commercial anime rendering.

## Scene and Composition

- Use clear silhouettes and expressive visual relationships, with framing and energy determined by the requested action.
- Build backgrounds from simplified graphic shapes and color masses.
- Use urban, abstract, or neon settings only when appropriate to the requested scene.
- Keep the environment supportive and no denser than the characters. No detailed scenery, realistic background painting, dense small decorations, or complex incidental objects.

## Style Boundaries and Output

- Keep anime-inspired facial design as the foundation; use Western web-cartoon influence for linework, shape exaggeration, acting, and graphic composition.
- Avoid drifting into generic Western animation mascots, default chibi proportions, polished gacha illustrations, or realistic character art.
- Produce a cohesive finished illustration with consistent bold outlines and cel shading.
- Add text, captions, speech balloons, or logos only when requested.
