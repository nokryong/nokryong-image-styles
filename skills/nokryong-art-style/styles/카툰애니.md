# Anime-Influenced Indie Web Cartoon

## Background Economy

- Keep the requested setting recognizable with only a few large, simple shapes. Leave broad quiet areas; do not fill empty space to make the image look complete.
- Include only explicitly requested objects or those indispensable to the action or location. Do not add incidental props, decorations, extra people, or repeated background objects.
- Keep background detail clearly lower than the subject. Do not subdivide large shapes into many small parts, patterns, surface textures, or decorative lighting effects. Preserve explicitly requested scene elements.

## Style and Request

- Combine appealing Japanese anime facial construction with the bold anatomical exaggeration and angular graphic language of Western TV cartoons. Use Panty & Stocking with Garterbelt as a stylistic reference for graphic construction.
- Follow the requested character identity, age, appearance, clothing, proportions, expression, action, props, camera angle, framing, and setting.
- References define character design; apply this illustration style consistently to every character.
- Keep the result playful, energetic, slightly edgy, and unmistakably two-dimensional.
- Low rendering density applies to all characters and scenery. Do not increase rendering complexity in response to elaborate character, costume, or scene descriptions.

## Anime Faces and Simple Mouths

- Use expressive anime-inspired eyes, softly simplified cheeks, expressive eyebrows, and small understated noses.
- Use large, open eyes with slightly upturned outer corners. Treat the upper eyelids and heavy eyelashes as dark graphic masses, extending the outer lashes into pointed triangular or wedge-shaped accents.
- Construct irises from simple color shapes with a few crisp highlights and little internal detail; avoid intricate patterns and excessive sparkle.
- Draw closed mouths as small lines and open mouths as flat shapes with minimal interior detail.
- Avoid separately modeled lips, protruding lip volumes, distinct upper and lower contours, lipstick, lip gloss, and realistic mouth shading.
- Keep facial appeal in eye design, expression, and proportions rather than detailed lip rendering.
- Preserve anime facial structure during exaggerated reactions; do not substitute elongated faces, heavily modeled cheekbones, or exaggerated Western cartoon mouth anatomy.
- Allow simple blush marks, tiny fangs, or playful mouth shapes where appropriate to the requested character and expression.

## Cartoon Proportions and Silhouette

- Make the head large relative to a vertically compressed torso. Keep the shoulders, rib cage, and lower face compact and simplified.
- Stretch the arms and legs noticeably relative to the torso, especially the forearms and lower legs. Keep limbs slender, tapering toward narrow wrists and ankles before connecting to oversized hands and footwear.
- Treat hands as large expressive shapes that communicate gesture and emotion. Build visible footwear as substantial geometric forms with thick soles and readable volume.
- Actively redesign the relative sizes of the head, torso, limbs, hands, and feet to create cartoon-driven proportions. Do not merely draw a tall fashion-model figure or shorten the limbs into chibi proportions.
- Preserve recognizable body-type differences, coherent joints, and readable gestures while applying this proportion system consistently.

## Shapes and Acting

- Construct forms from long straight sections, wedges, trapezoids, and angular curves. Introduce near-right-angle bends and clear directional changes in jawlines, hair tips, elbows, knees, collars, sleeves, and hems.
- Even flowing contours should combine straight sections with sharp corners. Avoid uniformly soft, rounded construction.
- Minimize muscle and joint descriptions; make actions readable through clear silhouettes and negative space between limbs and torso.
- Adapt exaggeration to the requested body type and pose rather than forcing one generic mascot design.
- Use lively gestures and readable facial acting, adjusting emotional intensity to the scene instead of imposing a fixed mischievous expression.
- Avoid realistic anatomical modeling and elongated fashion-illustration proportions.

## Bold Linework

- Use thick, crisp outlines in nearly black deep navy, with bold outer silhouettes and simpler, relatively thinner internal marks.
- Vary line weight to clarify expressions, gestures, and overlapping forms.
- Favor confident angular strokes, deliberate corners, and crisp screen-readable edges.
- Avoid delicate thin outlining, messy sketch marks, and excessive interior detail.

## Hair, Clothes, and Accessories

- Construct hair from a few large angular masses. Shape bangs as cleanly cut segments and side locks or longer sections as directional ribbons and wedges. No individual strands, fine hair texture, or excessive internal divisions.
- Preserve important hairstyles, clothing shapes, symbols, and accessories.
- Construct clothing from broad flat masses with only a few triangular or polygonal folds and broad shadow shapes. Apply the angular construction consistently to collars, sleeves, skirt pleats, socks, and shoes. No dense wrinkles, stitching, fabric texture, ornate patterns, or intricate accessory rendering; simplify essential design motifs.
- Keep detail density consistent across face, hair, clothing, and props.
- Avoid fine hair strands, elaborate gloss, intricate textures, and unnecessary ornament.

## Cel Shading and Color

- Use flat cel coloring and cel shading only: uniform base colors and a single shadow tone per material, arranged into a few broad hard-edged masses. Preserve substantial uninterrupted base-color areas; no intermediate tonal modeling.
- Design shadows as cut-out triangles, trapezoids, and polygons that clarify distinct planes of skin, hair, and clothing. Subtle violet or navy hues may unify shadow colors without overriding requested colors.
- Maintain depth through overlap, clear color separation, and purposeful shadow placement.
- Favor saturated colors and controlled graphic contrast, respecting requested character colors and scene palettes.
- Neon accents or colored rim light may be used when they fit the scene; render light on characters as simplified hard-edged shapes.
- Render background illumination with flat color areas and hard-edged graphic accents. Do not introduce blurred glow, gradients, or soft shading in the background.
- Avoid airbrushing, painterly blending, realistic skin texture, complex tonal modeling, and glossy commercial anime rendering.

## Scene and Composition

- Use clear silhouettes and expressive visual relationships, with framing and energy determined by the requested action.
- Use natural asymmetry and, where appropriate to the action, a gentle S-shaped flow through the angular limbs. Match the pose and expression to the requested emotion; do not impose a playful expression or a fixed stance.
- Apply exaggerated proportions to the parts visible within the requested crop. Do not widen the framing or force a full-body view to display hands, feet, or footwear.
- Build backgrounds from simplified geometric masses with the same angular construction and flat cel shading, using less contrast and detail than on the characters.
- Use urban, abstract, or neon settings only when appropriate to the requested scene.
- Keep the environment supportive and no denser than the characters. No detailed scenery, realistic background painting, dense small decorations, or complex incidental objects.

## Style Boundaries and Output

- Keep anime-inspired facial design as the foundation; use Western web-cartoon influence for linework, shape exaggeration, acting, and graphic composition.
- Avoid drifting into generic Western animation mascots, default chibi proportions, polished gacha illustrations, or realistic character art.
- Produce a cohesive finished illustration with consistent bold outlines and cel shading.
- Add text, captions, speech balloons, or logos only when requested.
- Style priority: thick angular linework, compact torso and elongated limbs, oversized visible hands and footwear, heavy eyelashes and slightly upturned eye corners, then clear flat cel shading. Preserve explicit character and composition requests.
