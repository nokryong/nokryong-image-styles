# Low-Detail Amateur Webcomic

## Background Economy

- Keep the requested setting recognizable with only a few large, simple shapes. Leave broad quiet areas; do not fill empty space to make the image look complete.
- Include only explicitly requested objects or those indispensable to the action or location. Do not add incidental props, decorations, extra people, or repeated background objects.
- Keep background detail clearly lower than the subject. Do not subdivide large shapes into many small parts, patterns, surface textures, or decorative lighting effects. Preserve explicitly requested scene elements.

## Style and Request

- Draw like a webtoon aspirant casually posting a simple hobby comic to an online community.
- Follow the requested character, clothing, expression, action, camera angle, framing, and setting.
- Keep the drawing casual, readable, and lightly drawn, with simple shapes and very few lines.
- Low detail density is mandatory. No ultra-detailed or premium illustration finish, even when the requested character or scene is elaborate.

## Deformation Level

- Use the shared moderate deformation level: a visibly but modestly enlarged head, a compact simplified lower face, and mildly shortened, simplified torso and limbs. Keep joints and gestures coherent.
- Keep this degree of proportion exaggeration the same across the standard illustration styles, including commercial vector: stronger than the light deformation of everyday webtoon, clearly milder than deformed manga, and far milder than chibi. Each style retains its own facial design and rendering technique.

## Character and Shape

- Use a simply drawn face with minimal eyes, nose, and mouth. Convey expression without detailed eye construction or decorative eyelashes.
- Keep the drawing casual and slightly awkward while preserving coherent body structure within the shared moderate deformation level.
- Draw hair as a simple silhouette with only the divisions needed to recognize the hairstyle. No individual strands or fine internal lines.
- Draw clothing through its outline and only indispensable structural marks. Omit routine fold lines, stitching, textures, and small accessory details; simplify hands and anatomy as well.

## Linework

- Use a few plain black hand-drawn lines with slight natural unevenness. Do not refine them into detailed manga inking or polished professional line art.
- Keep only marks needed for recognition, expression, action, or essential overlap. Leave other surfaces blank.
- No fine detail lines, decorative line-weight work, hatching, construction lines, or repeated corrective strokes.

## Color and Shading

- Use a black-and-white base with one flat accent color for selective coloring. Apply that color where it suits the character or scene, not only to the irises.
- Ignore requested hues in monochrome areas. Render dark local colors as solid black and light local colors as unfilled white; do not convert colors into gray values.
- Fills describe the object's own color, not lighting. Do not add form shadows, highlights, or intermediate tones to model volume. Use contours or small white gaps to keep black shapes readable.
- The single chosen accent color is the only exception, and may selectively replace a black or white fill. Do not add shades of that accent color, other hues, gray fills, screentones, gradients, textures, or glossy rendering.

## Scene and Output

- Use broad white areas and only the simplest location cues needed for the scene. No complex backgrounds, dense linework, decorative texture, or complex incidental objects.
- Adapt perspective and composition to the action while maintaining low detail density.
- Produce one webcomic scene by default. Include lettering, dialogue, signs, or other readable text only when requested.
