Soft Hand-Drawn SD Chibi
Background Economy
- Keep the background plain and quiet. Use white, very light gray, or another low-contrast simple backdrop unless a specific setting is requested.
- When a setting is needed, describe it with only a few large simple shapes. Do not fill the image with props, decorations, patterns, or repeated background objects.
- Keep all background detail clearly weaker than the character. The character must dominate the image immediately.
Style and Request
- Create a soft, hand-drawn super-deformed anime illustration based on a cute Korean/Japanese hobby-drawing SD style.
- The appeal comes from simplified shape language, soft rounded forms, slightly uneven handmade linework, and expressive posing.
- Follow the requested character identity, clothing, expression, action, camera angle, framing, and setting. References define identity; this document defines rendering.
- Do not upgrade the drawing into a polished commercial anime illustration, a glossy game render, or a generic modern chibi style.
Proportions and Construction
- Use a strongly super-deformed body, usually around 2 heads tall, but do not rigidly force every figure into an exact 2.0-head formula. Depending on pose and camera, allow a flexible range around roughly 1.8 to 2.4 heads.
- The head is very large and visually dominant. In an ordinary standing full-body view, the head itself (not counting long twin-tails or other hair extensions) is close in height to the short torso and legs together. The body remains small and compact; do not solve the ratio by enlarging hair alone.
- Keep this extreme head-to-body contrast even with complex costumes, long hair, close crops, or dynamic poses. Never drift toward a merely big-headed, normally proportioned anime figure.
- The neck is nearly absent or extremely short.
- The torso and pelvis are simplified into one soft compact mass rather than realistic ribcage-and-hip anatomy.
- Arms and legs are short, rounded, and tube-like. Hands and feet are tiny and simplified. Shorten the actual limbs beneath the costume too: avoid long calves, adult-like thighs, developed waists, and natural-sized hands hidden inside small clothes.
- Elbows, knees, shoulders, and hips are not described with realistic anatomy. Keep the whole body toy-like, soft, and readable.
Head and Face Shape
- The head is large and soft, with a rounded top but a broad, slightly squared-off lower facial silhouette. It is neither a perfect circle nor an elongated anime oval. The face stays wide across the cheeks instead of tapering neatly toward a point.
- The cheeks are one of the key features. The lower sides of the face contour swell outward into two short, rounded cheek bulges just beneath or beside the eyes, then turn inward toward a very short chin. They should remain visible even without blush; they are not separate balloon shapes, blush disks, or a conventional tapered anime jaw.
- The jaw is tiny and understated. Avoid a sharp chin or mature lower-face structure.
- Place the face relatively low on the head so the large forehead and hair mass remain visible.
Eyes and Facial Features
- The eyes are the signature feature of the style.
- When the eyes are open, draw them conspicuously oversized relative to the visible face, not merely large relative to a normal anime face. Each eye occupies roughly a quarter of the face width, with a rounded lower contour; the pair dominates the central-lower face. Use a clear dark upper eyelid curve, heavier than the minimal inner details but not a broad rectangular bar.
- Keep the eyes structurally simple: the colored iris is very large and broadly circular, filling much of the visible eye opening, with only a small clear sclera region around or above it. Place a conspicuous, simple white highlight high within the iris. The eye should read as a big round colored shape, not a standard anime eye built from a small iris sitting in a wide white almond. A half-closed lid is used only for expressions that request it.
- Construct each iris from a few broad, sharply separated color areas, often a darker upper area and lighter lower area. Keep the white highlights as simple distinct shapes rather than elaborate reflections.
- Eye openness, iris position, and the upper-lid curve must respond to the requested expression. Wide, sparkling eyes are as important as sleepy, half-lidded eyes; never impose one fixed expression on every character.
- Do not add complex iris rings, textured gradients, detailed eyelashes, or glossy high-end anime eye rendering.
- Eyebrows are tiny, sparse, and simple, but important for expression.
- Omit the nose completely in all views and expressions. Do not draw any nose dot, short stroke, nostril, nose bridge, nose contour, or nose shadow. Keep the central area between the eyes and mouth unmarked.
- The mouth uses a simple outline or single fill and changes shape with the requested emotion: it can be a tiny mark, small curve, or openly rounded laughing/shouting mouth. Do not lock every character into the same tiny smile.
- Add small blush marks or blush dots beneath the eyes where appropriate. Keep blush simple and graphic.
Hair
- Build the hair from a few large rounded masses and chunky locks. Keep its major silhouette, bangs, and signature accessories recognizable, but do not turn every lock into a separate carefully rendered anime strand.
- Avoid detailed strand rendering or realistic hair texture.
- Keep the outer silhouette soft, slightly irregular, and handmade rather than vector-perfect.
- Bangs and side locks should be simple and readable, not finely subdivided.
- Twin-tails, bobs, and other hairstyles should remain thick, soft, and compact.
Clothing and Props
- Simplify clothing into broad cute shapes with only a few necessary folds. Costumes retain identity but must follow the tiny body and short limbs; do not restore realistic shoulder width, waist length, thigh volume, tailored seams, or long sleeves simply because the outfit is detailed.
- Keep accessories readable but not intricate.
- Do not add surface texture, stitching, fabric realism, or decorative density unless specifically required.
- Hands may become mitten-like simplified forms when detail is unnecessary.
- Props should also be simplified into large clear shapes.
Pose and Body Flow
- This style depends heavily on body flow. Avoid stiff, symmetrical, flat front-facing standing poses unless explicitly requested.
- For an action or relaxed stance, establish a gentle directional flow through the head and body; do not impose a C-curve or S-curve where the requested pose does not need one.
- Tilt the head and body slightly to create rhythm.
- Shift body weight clearly to one side when standing or moving.
- Separate the near side and far side of the body. Show front/back depth through overlap.
- Overlap is important: a hand may overlap the cheek, one arm may cross the torso, one leg may come forward while the other recedes.
- When posing seated or crouched figures, make the body feel tucked, rounded, and flowing rather than blocky.
- Even simple poses should have a sense of softness, direction, and asymmetry.
- Avoid mannequin-like symmetry, flat mirrored limbs, and rigid upright balance.
Linework
- Use clear dark pen lines, often deep navy, black-brown, or plum as appropriate to the character's palette. The color itself is not a fixed palette requirement.
- Linework should feel like pen drawing: smooth, rounded, and confident, but slightly uneven in a natural handmade way.
- Use a moderately assertive outer contour and lighter, economical interior marks. Retain mild natural hand-drawn variation; neither extremely thick sticker outlines nor hair-thin polished anime lines.
- Outer contours should be clear and readable. Interior detail lines should remain sparse.
- Do not use mechanical vector-perfect curves or dense interior detailing.
Color and Shading
- Preserve the requested character's own local colors. Use clear, lightly softened color blocks with enough saturation and contrast to keep shapes distinct; do not impose an overall washed-out pastel filter on unrelated characters.
- Keep fills broad and clean.
- Shading should be very simple and limited. Use a small number of crisp, easily distinguished shadow shapes rather than translucent cloudy shading across the face, clothes, or hair.
- Do not use complex rendering, realistic light logic, painterly blending, textured shading, or glossy skin treatment.
- Hair and eyes may contain a few well-placed, simple solid highlights, but not many shiny streaks, elaborate gloss, or painterly reflected light.
- Skin should be pale and soft. Blush should stay simple and graphic.
- The overall result should feel matte, light, and cute rather than glossy or dramatic.
Composition and Output
- Compose the image so the whole silhouette reads quickly and clearly.
- Follow the requested crop, whether face close-up, half body, full body, or multiple figures. In any crop, the same oversized eye-to-face relationship and cheek-led head construction must remain visible; do not change styles between crops.
- Keep the figure readable first; background and props are secondary.
- Use text, captions, speech balloons, or logos only when explicitly requested.
- Preserve the playful, soft, hand-drawn mood throughout the image. As a consistency check, if the face reads as a standard small-eyed cute anime portrait on a shortened body, the construction is still wrong: enlarge and round the eyes relative to the visible face, restore the projecting cheek contours, and compact the limbs before adding polish.
Avoid
- Generic polished modern chibi style
- Realistic anatomy or semi-realistic body structure
- Long legs or elongated limbs
- Sharp jawlines or mature face structure
- Any visible nose, including tiny dots, short dashes, nostrils, or nose-bridge shading
- Detailed anime eyes with layered irises and dense eyelashes
- Strong glossy rendering
- Complex lighting
- Painterly shading or airbrush rendering
- Symmetrical mannequin poses
- Over-detailed clothing or accessories
- Vector-clean sterile linework
- Pure black heavy manga inking as the default
