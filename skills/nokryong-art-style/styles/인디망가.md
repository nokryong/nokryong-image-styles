# Minimalist Rough Indie Manga — Extreme Detail & Object Economy

## Background Economy

- Keep the requested setting recognizable with only a few large, simple shapes. Leave broad quiet areas; do not fill empty space to make the image look complete.
- Include only explicitly requested objects or those indispensable to the action or location. Do not add incidental props, decorations, extra people, or repeated background objects.
- Keep background detail clearly lower than the subject. Do not subdivide large shapes into many small parts, patterns, surface textures, or decorative lighting effects. Preserve explicitly requested scene elements.

## Document Purpose

- Create highly simplified, spontaneous black-and-white Japanese indie manga drawings with exceptionally low detail density.
- Preserve expressive character acting, recognizable silhouettes, and charming handmade imperfections using the fewest necessary marks.
- **The defining quality is deliberate omission, not roughness, decorative linework, or visual complexity.**
- The drawing should feel as though the artist deliberately skipped everything that was not absolutely necessary.

## User Request Priority

- Follow the user's explicit requests for character identity, gender, age, appearance, hairstyle, clothing, pose, action, expression, props, camera angle, composition, and setting.
- This document controls visual simplification, detail density, object economy, linework, hatching, and rendering.
- Preserve explicitly requested elements while reducing their visual construction to the simplest recognizable forms.
- Never invent additional details, accessories, props, environmental elements, or decorative features to enrich the image.
- **Character identity and requested actions take priority over detail. Everything else is expendable.**

## Absolute Simplification Priority

**Apply these rules before drawing any element.**

- Include only what is essential for recognizing the subject, understanding the pose, and reading the expression.
- Remove secondary details even when they would normally improve the appearance of the illustration.
- When a detail can be removed without harming recognition, remove it.
- When an object is not explicitly requested or essential to the specified action, omit it entirely.
- When choosing between simplifying an element and eliminating it, prefer elimination whenever possible.
- Never compensate for simplified drawing with additional texture, shading, decoration, or background objects.
- Do not attempt to make the image feel complete through added visual information.
- **Empty space and omitted details are intentional finished results, not areas requiring further rendering.**

## Core Visual Identity

- Draw like a casual, independently made Japanese monochrome gag manga or personal webcomic.
- Prioritize instantly readable silhouettes, appealing expressions, and loose character acting.
- Use a fine fineliner pen for thin, slightly irregular hand-drawn lines.
- Build each character from a small number of large, simple shapes.
- Keep light-colored surfaces blank and dark-colored surfaces as simple solid-black shapes.
- Favor loose, economical drawing decisions over polished construction.
- Preserve an informal, spontaneous appearance without adding artificial sketchiness.
- The result must look quickly and confidently drawn, not meticulously illustrated and then artificially simplified.

## Strict Character Detail Economy

- Construct the head from one simple, slightly irregular outer silhouette.
- Use a short, rounded anime face with a small lower face and chin, and comparatively large, simply drawn eyes.
- Preserve the anime facial structure and expression; simplify only the internal detail of the eyes, nose, and mouth, and omit unnecessary lines.
- Simplify eyes into basic manga shapes with extremely limited internal detail.
- Use tiny, economical noses and simple mouths.
- Express emotion primarily through eye shape, eyebrow position, mouth shape, and head posture.
- Avoid decorative eyelashes, elaborate irises, elaborate highlights, cheek rendering, and extra facial marks.
- Draw hair as a few large, continuous shapes.
- Do not depict individual hair strands, layered locks, small flyaways, or internal hair texture.
- Preserve recognizable hairstyles through their overall silhouette and essential divisions.
- Reduce hands to simple readable forms with minimal finger separation.
- Suggest anatomy through silhouette rather than internal construction.
- Preserve appealing character proportions without anatomical elaboration.

**Do not add lines merely because the corresponding anatomical feature exists.**

## Clothing and Accessory Reduction

- Preserve the recognizable category and silhouette of explicitly requested clothing.
- Represent garments using their outer boundaries and only indispensable structural marks.
- Reduce collars, seams, cuffs, belts, and folds to the minimum needed for recognition.
- Leave light-colored clothing white; fill dark-colored clothing with a simple solid black.
- Omit minor seams, stitching, buttons, zippers, pockets, fabric texture, and decorative trim unless essential to identifying the requested design.
- Do not invent clothing decorations or accessory details.
- Simplify complex outfits into major recognizable shapes rather than reproducing all their components.
- Accessories explicitly required by the user must remain recognizable but should be constructed with very few lines.

**Visual recognition matters. Costume completeness does not.**

## Extreme Object Suppression

**Default object count: zero additional objects.**

- Draw no unrequested props, furniture, tools, decorations, or environmental objects.
- Do not add cups, books, plants, tables, chairs, windows, shelves, lamps, or miscellaneous background items to make a scene feel natural.
- Do not introduce objects to communicate personality, mood, profession, or narrative context.
- Do not fill empty areas with visual storytelling elements.
- Do not invent additional accessories, handheld items, or surrounding objects.
- If an object is explicitly requested, draw that object in its simplest recognizable form.
- If several objects are requested, preserve them but avoid adding smaller secondary components.
- Do not create supporting objects merely to justify a character's pose.
- An otherwise empty image containing only the requested character is a fully satisfactory result.

**No unsolicited objects. No environmental decoration. No visual clutter.**

## Background and Composition

- Default to a completely empty white background.
- Do not add a floor, horizon line, ground shadow, wall texture, scenery, or architectural framing unless explicitly requested.
- Avoid environmental context when the pose and expression already communicate the scene.
- If a setting is requested, represent it using only its most indispensable identifying shapes.
- Do not invent additional environmental objects within a requested setting.
- Keep extensive uninterrupted white negative space.
- Respect the user's requested camera angle, subject scale, crop, and framing.
- Allow asymmetric compositions and unfinished visual boundaries.
- Do not add speech bubbles, lettering, captions, decorative symbols, or panel embellishments unless requested.

## Minimal Linework

- Use a small number of thin, slightly shaky black pen strokes.
- Favor one imperfect contour over multiple corrective lines.
- Avoid construction lines, overlapping sketch strokes, and redundant contours.
- Do not trace every visible edge of an object.
- Allow minor forms to disappear into blank paper.
- Omit unnecessary internal divisions even when they would make the drawing more technically accurate.
- Use visual shorthand instead of complete shape descriptions.
- Keep silhouettes legible without refining them.

**Every line must have a clear purpose: recognition, expression, action, or essential overlap.**

## Restrained Vertical Hatching

- Preserve the distinctive handmade vertical parallel-line hatching of minimalist indie manga.
- Use a few hatch marks only for essential emotional shorthand, never for shading or converting colors into tonal fields.
- Prefer no hatching over unnecessary hatching.
- Keep hatch groups sparse, informal, and clearly separated by white gaps.
- Avoid filling entire hair masses or clothing sections with dense strokes.
- Do not use hatching as decoration or substitute it for omitted details.
- Never render individual hair strands through hatching.
- Do not add hatching merely to balance the composition.
- Preserve broad white areas without overriding dark local-color fills.
- Avoid extensive crosshatching, gradients, screentones, and tonal modeling. Solid-black local-color fills are allowed.

**Hatching is an occasional visual accent, never a requirement to fill a surface.**

## Expression and Character Acting

- Concentrate the drawing's expressive information in the eyes, eyebrows, mouth, and silhouette.
- Prefer recognizable emotional shorthand over detailed facial construction.
- Allow exaggerated comic reactions through a small number of strong visual changes.
- Use only essential motion lines or emotional marks.
- Do not decorate expressions with unnecessary symbols or effects.
- Preserve charm and personality through simplicity, asymmetry, and spontaneous gesture.
- Let a single well-placed facial mark communicate what several elaborate marks otherwise would.

## Monochrome Rendering

- Ignore requested hues in monochrome areas. Render dark local colors as solid black and light local colors as unfilled white; do not convert colors into gray values.
- Fills describe the object's own color, not lighting. Do not add form shadows, highlights, or intermediate tones to model volume. Use contours or small white gaps to keep black shapes readable.
- Use only pure black marks and fills on pure white paper; no gray fills or tinted paper.
- No grayscale gradients, soft shadows, painterly rendering, dimensional lighting, or realistic material effects.
- No elaborate shading or polished commercial manga finish.
- Maintain a flat, airy, unmistakably two-dimensional drawing.

## Mandatory Omission Rules

Before finalizing the drawing, conceptually remove:

- All objects not explicitly requested or essential to the action.
- All background elements not essential to the specified setting.
- All hair detail beyond the defining silhouette.
- All clothing lines that do not materially explain the garment.
- All redundant contour and anatomical lines.
- All decorative marks and unnecessary surface textures.
- All shading that does not improve immediate readability.
- All unnecessary environmental storytelling.
- All minor visual information that can disappear without changing the scene's meaning.

**If removing something does not damage recognition, expression, or the user's explicit instructions, it should not appear in the finished image.**

## Prohibited Elements

- No polished anime illustration or commercial manga finish.
- No intricate eyes, eyelashes, facial anatomy, or realistic facial rendering.
- No individual hair strands or detailed hair texture.
- No elaborate clothing folds, tiny accessories, ornamental seams, or fabric patterns.
- No additional props or background decorations.
- No unnecessary furniture, architecture, or environmental objects.
- No excessive sketch marks, dense hatching, crosshatching, or decorative texture.
- No visual embellishment intended to make a simple drawing feel richer.
- No photorealism, 3D rendering, cinematic lighting, painterly effects, or gradients.
- No artificial complexity disguised as handmade roughness.

## Output Rules

- Produce a final monochrome 2D indie manga image following the user's requested character and scene.
- Preserve recognizable character identity, pose, expression, and explicitly requested elements.
- Keep visual information exceptionally sparse.
- Default to a character-focused drawing with no additional objects and no background scenery.
- Prefer large blank areas, extremely economical contours, and minimal hatching.
- Do not add detail simply because the image appears too empty.
- Never interpret simplicity as a lack of completion.

**Final Priority: Recognition > Expression > Essential Structure > Everything Else.**

**When in doubt, draw less. When something is optional, leave it out.**
